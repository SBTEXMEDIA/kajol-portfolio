Service Overview

Workbooks People Actually
Fill In

"An exercise nobody completes is not a teaching failure. It is usually a layout failure."

SBTEXMEDIA designs professional workbooks for course creators, coaches, trainers, therapists and educators. Led by Bayazid Kajol, founder of SBTEXMEDIA, this service covers course and training workbooks, coaching exercise books, student worksheets and handouts, guided journals and planners — delivered as printable versions and, where useful, as fillable PDFs people can type directly into.

Workbook design is the only one of these disciplines where the reader is expected to do something, and that changes everything about the layout. A book is designed to be read; a workbook is designed to be worked in. The measure of success is not whether it looks good on a sales page — it is whether someone picks up a pen, answers question one, and keeps going to question fourteen. Almost everything that goes wrong in a workbook is a barrier to that.

The barriers are consistent and fixable. Answer space that is three lines tall for a question requiring a paragraph, so the writer either cramps their handwriting or gives up. Every exercise looking identical, so a two-minute reflection and a forty-minute planning task appear equally daunting. Instructions written for the author rather than the person doing the work. Sections that open with the hardest exercise, killing momentum on page two. Getting these right is unglamorous, entirely learnable, and the difference between a workbook that gets completed and one that gets abandoned after module one. Where the piece is read rather than worked in, see ebook design; where it is a short free download, see lead magnet design.

The Real Cost

Why Professional Workbook Design
Matters

Course completion rates are a marketing problem long before anyone admits it. The workbook is where they are usually lost.

Everyone selling a course, programme or coaching package eventually confronts the same uncomfortable number: how many people who bought it actually finished it. Completion matters commercially, not just pedagogically. People who finish leave testimonials, refer others, and buy the next thing. People who stall in module two do none of that, and they quietly become refund requests and non-renewals. The workbook is frequently where that stalling happens, and it is rarely blamed because it looks fine.

The most common failure is answer space. A question asking someone to describe their ideal client in detail, followed by three ruled lines, tells the writer their real answer is unwelcome. Handwriting takes roughly two to three times the space that typed text occupies, and workbooks laid out on screen consistently underestimate it. The person either compresses their thinking to fit or skips the exercise, and either way the value of doing it is lost.

Visual uniformity is the second failure. When a quick two-minute check-in and a substantial planning exercise are presented identically, the learner cannot judge what they are committing to. Faced with that ambiguity they defer — they will "do the workbook properly at the weekend" — and the weekend does not arrive. Varying the visual weight of exercises by the effort they require lets people slot the short ones into small gaps, which is how workbooks actually get completed in real life.

Sequencing matters just as much. Opening a module with its hardest exercise is a reliable way to lose people, because nobody has built momentum yet. Starting with something quick and satisfying, then escalating, produces measurably more completions of the difficult work later. This costs nothing to implement and is almost never done, because authors naturally order material by logical structure rather than by psychological load.

Finally, format access. Some of your audience will print; a significant share will not own a printer at all and will simply not engage with a print-only workbook. Others find typing into fields far less intimidating than a blank ruled page. Supplying both printable and fillable versions removes a barrier that has nothing to do with your teaching and everything to do with the file you handed them.

Sector Experience

Industries Served

Twelve-plus sectors, each with different expectations of how a learner should be guided through the work.

Workbooks carry sector conventions that matter more than they first appear. Corporate training typically expects structured, time-boxed exercises with clear outputs a manager can review. Coaching and wellness materials tend toward open reflection with generous unstructured space. Classroom worksheets need instructions pitched precisely at a reading level and answer areas sized for the age group using them. Regulated training may need to evidence completion for compliance purposes, which changes how pages are structured entirely.

How It Works

Our Process

The same six steps on a four-page worksheet set and a 120-page course workbook.

There is one step here that no other design service needs: I complete the exercises myself, by hand, on a printed draft. It sounds excessive and it is the fastest way to discover that question seven needs twice the space it was given, that two exercises ask essentially the same thing, and that the instruction on page nine is ambiguous until you actually try to follow it.

1
Exercise Audit
I read every exercise and estimate how much writing each genuinely requires, flagging prompts that are ambiguous, duplicated or sequenced in a way that will lose people. You get that feedback before any quote, and it is often the most useful part of the project.
2
Written Scope & Fixed Quote
You receive a written summary covering page count, whether fillable and printable versions are both included, binding and print preparation, revision rounds and a delivery date, at a fixed price. Nothing starts until you approve it.
3
Exercise System & Sample Module
A visual system is built for each exercise type — quick check-in, reflection, planning grid, assessment — so effort becomes visible at a glance. One full module is designed and approved before the rest is laid out.
4
Full Layout & Sequencing
The complete workbook is built to the approved system, with modules opening on lighter exercises to build momentum, instructions rewritten from the learner's point of view, and progress markers added where they help people see how far they have come.
5
Print, Fill and Complete Testing
The workbook is printed on A4 and US Letter and the exercises are physically completed by hand to verify the space works. The fillable version is tested for tab order and field sizing, and the whole thing is checked in grayscale in case someone prints without colour.
6
Delivery & Handover
You receive printable and fillable PDFs, a binding-ready version with gutter allowance if you are printing physically, and the editable source files so you can add a module next year without starting over.
