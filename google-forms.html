Service Overview

Google Forms That Collect
Data You Can Actually Use

"Anyone can build a Google Form in ten minutes. The ten minutes is exactly the problem."

SBTEXMEDIA designs professional Google Forms for businesses that need information collected cleanly and delivered somewhere useful without anyone tidying it up afterwards. Led by Bayazid Kajol, founder of SBTEXMEDIA, this service covers client intake forms, business enquiry forms, surveys, self-marking quizzes, registration forms, feedback forms and order forms — together with the Google Sheets structure and automation that sits behind them.

Google Forms is genuinely easy to start with, and that is precisely why so many business forms built in it collect messy data. Questions get written in the order they occurred to someone rather than the order a respondent can answer them. Free-text fields are used where a dropdown belongs, so the same city arrives spelled four different ways. Nothing is validated, so phone numbers come back as "call me". Every respondent sees every question, including the twenty that do not apply to them. And the response spreadsheet is left exactly as Google generated it, which means somebody reformats it by hand every week.

I build forms as data-collection instruments. Question order follows how people actually think, conditional branching means respondents only see what applies to them, response validation catches errors at the point of entry, and the linked Google Sheet is structured with proper columns, formulas and summaries so it is useful the moment a response lands. Where it helps, Apps Script automation handles notifications, auto-responses and PDF generation. And where a fillable PDF, Microsoft Forms or Jotform would serve you better, I will say so.

The Real Cost

Why Professional Google Forms
Matter

The form is free. Cleaning up what it collects is not.

The trap with Google Forms is that the barrier to entry is almost zero. Anyone in the business can build one in a lunch break, which means forms multiply without anyone designing them. Six months later a company typically has fourteen forms, four of which duplicate each other, none of which validate anything, and all of which feed spreadsheets that somebody manually tidies before they are usable.

The cost lands downstream, in the spreadsheet. When a form does not restrict what people can type, the same answer arrives in a dozen variations — "London", "london", "Londin", "Greater London", "LDN". You cannot filter that, count it, or build a chart from it without cleaning it first, and that cleaning happens every single week forever. A dropdown at the form stage eliminates the problem permanently in about ninety seconds of setup.

Length is the second silent cost. Forms that show every question to every respondent get abandoned partway through, and the people who abandon them never tell you. A twenty-eight-question form with proper branching often becomes an eight-question experience for most respondents, which changes the completion rate substantially. You do not see that improvement as a complaint being resolved — you see it as more submissions arriving than before.

Then there is the follow-up cost. Without automation, someone has to check the spreadsheet, notice a new submission, email the respondent to confirm receipt, and notify whichever colleague owns the next step. That is a small task repeated endlessly. Apps Script can send the confirmation, alert the right person, generate a PDF summary and file it, all within seconds of submission and without anyone remembering to look.

Finally, presentation matters more than people expect for a free tool. A form carrying your logo, brand colours, a clear header image and properly written questions signals a business that pays attention. A default purple form with a typo in question three signals the opposite — and it is often the first interaction a new client has with you.

How It Works

Our Process

The same six steps on a five-question feedback form and a branching registration system.

Form projects go wrong in a predictable way: everyone agrees the questions, nobody discusses what the answers are for, and the finished form collects data that cannot answer the question the business actually had. I work backwards from the spreadsheet instead — deciding what the data needs to look like first, then designing the questions that produce it.

1
Discovery & Output Mapping
Before quoting I ask what decision this data will inform, who reads the spreadsheet, what needs to happen automatically after a submission, and which fields must be controlled rather than free text. Designing from the output backwards prevents almost every common form failure.
2
Written Scope & Fixed Quote
You receive a written summary listing every question, the branching logic, validation rules, spreadsheet structure and any automation, with a delivery date and fixed price. Nothing starts until you approve it.
3
Question Design & Sequencing
Questions are rewritten for clarity and ordered the way people actually think — easy identifying details first, demanding questions once they are committed, sensitive questions last. Free text is replaced with controlled choices wherever the data allows.
4
Build, Branch & Brand
The form is built with sections, conditional go-to logic, validation rules and custom error messages, then branded with your logo, colours, header artwork and typography. The linked Google Sheet is structured with formatted columns, formulas and summaries.
5
Automation & Live Testing
Apps Script automation is added where useful, then the form is completed end to end on desktop and mobile — every branch path taken in both directions, every validation rule triggered, and real test submissions checked in the spreadsheet before the test rows are cleared.
6
Handover & Aftercare
Ownership is transferred to your Google account, sharing permissions are configured correctly, and you receive a short note explaining how to edit questions, adjust branching and read the summaries yourself. Questions are answered long after the invoice closes.
